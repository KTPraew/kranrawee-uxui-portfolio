Case Study

Invoice Management Redesign

Redesigning an internal invoice system without breaking the workflows people already knew.

Role
UX/UI Designer
Timeline
~1 month of active work, spread across 3-4 months
Year
2025
Tools
  • Figma

Problem

The legacy invoice system was cluttered and slow to work with. The back-office team needed something that felt modern, but not so different that they'd have to relearn their day-to-day work.

Approach

  • I removed functions nobody used to cut the clutter, instead of adding more.
  • I reorganized the information architecture so related data lived together and was easier to scan.
  • I kept every change close to the workflows the team already knew, so adoption wouldn't be a fight.

Before / After

Supervisor Dashboard

Before, tasks were scattered across separate menus with no single view. After, I added a supervisor dashboard that connects those menus together, with indicators showing what the team is working on.

Confirmation Modals

Before, critical actions like deleting or submitting an invoice happened with one click, no way back. After, I added confirmation modals for those actions to prevent costly mistakes.

Microcopy

Before, labels and instructions used internal jargon that didn't match how the team actually talked about their work. After, I rewrote the microcopy to match their real vocabulary.

Suppliers & Units

Before, supplier and unit data lived in separate, inconsistent layouts. After, I standardized both into the same component pattern, so switching between them felt familiar.

Reflection

A few things stuck with me from this project. Defining scope early mattered more than I expected, it's easy for a redesign to quietly grow into a rebuild. Working solo pushed me to adapt fast without a team to lean on. Redesigning something people already knew taught me to respect their comfort zone, not just chase what looked better on paper. Working closely with developers also meant simplifying components with implementation in mind, not just visual polish. And on timeline, this project also taught me to set expectations early with stakeholders. What should have taken a month stretched to three or four, mostly waiting on requirements and reviews. Next time, I'd build that buffer into the plan from day one instead of discovering it along the way.
