Case Study

Smart Payment

Making government payments fast, transparent, and easy to trust.

Role
UX/UI Designer, from discovery through developer handoff
Timeline
1–2 months
Year
2026
Tools
Figma Lucid
Problem Research Key Decisions Design System Solution Testing Results
01 · Problem

The legacy process for paying the Industrial Estate Authority of Thailand was complicated, slow, and left people unsure whether their payment had even gone through.

70%
of users couldn't find their invoice details
60%
said payments took too long
02 · Research & Discovery

I interviewed stakeholders, mapped out the existing payment workflow to find where people got stuck, and looked at both government service standards and fintech products to see what "trustworthy and fast" actually looks like. A few numbers stood out along the way.

85%
of users said they preferred platforms that felt fast and transparent
90%
expected more than one way to pay, QR codes and mobile banking especially
03 · Key Decisions
01
I built a consistent design system (color, type, spacing, tokens) from the start, since this needed to scale across multiple payment types, not just one screen.
02
I let users select and pay multiple invoices at once, instead of forcing one transaction at a time, since that's closer to how people actually manage their bills.
03
I split the invoice details screen into two columns, invoice details on one side, payment method on the other, with fees shown in real time, so nothing felt hidden until the last step.
04 · Design System

To keep the interface consistent as it scaled, I built out a lightweight design system alongside the UI colors, typography, spacing, and reusable components starting from early sketches on paper.

Early sketches working out the invoice table and CTA placement on paper first.

I also defined design tokens for color, typography, spacing, and border radius, so designers and developers could stay in sync without re-explaining values screen by screen.

06 · Testing & Iteration

In usability testing, people got confused about why fees differed between payment methods, and some hesitated right before confirming. I made the fee breakdown clearer, tightened the visual flow leading up to confirmation, and made the final call-to-action harder to miss.

Before
After
07 · Results

By the end of testing, the confusion around fees had eased and people moved through the flow with more confidence.

The clearest lesson: in a high-stakes flow like paying money to the government, information architecture and visual hierarchy aren't just nice to have, they're what makes someone trust the "Pay" button enough to press it.
Reflection

This project reinforced how much clarity matters when money is involved. People don't need more features, they need to feel certain about what's about to happen before they commit.
