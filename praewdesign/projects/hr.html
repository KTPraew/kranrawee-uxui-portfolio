HR Management System

Human resources system for Subdistrict Administrative Organizations (SAO)

It turns complex civil service rules and regulations into a UI that works in real daily use.

Client
Stella Plus
Role
UX/UI Designer
Tool
Figma
Year
2024
Project time
About 3 months (Design phase)
Status
Completed and in use

Problem

Civil service HR follows strict and complex rules and regulations. Records specific to civil service, such as position history, rank, and royal decorations, sit alongside everyday HR data. The challenge was to turn these rules into a UI that works in real daily use.

Process

I worked with a design team, and we split the work by module. Over about 3 months of design, we focused on translating civil service regulations into a usable UI.

Define

Before designing screens, I mapped the structure of the system: login, organization, three roles (System Admin, HR Admin, and Employee), and five modules covering manpower, employee records, the organization chart, payroll, and benefits.

Design system

Key decisions

Three decisions shaped how the rules show up in the UI.

1

Step-by-step progress tracker

Workflows with many steps need a clear sense of progress. A step-by-step tracker shows what is done, what is pending, and what comes next.

2

Tabbed employee profile

An employee profile holds a lot of information. Splitting it into tabs, such as royal decorations and important documents, lets users go straight to the section they need.

Profile

Royal decorations

Important documents

3

Rules-based configuration

Welfare and leave eligibility depend on rules. Admins set them as rules in the system, and the settings stay easy to manage.

Solution

The main screens of the system.

Results

The system is complete and in use at a Subdistrict Administrative Organization.

Reflection

Designing for government workflows means understanding the rules, not just making the UI look better. When it works, the interface disappears behind the rules.
